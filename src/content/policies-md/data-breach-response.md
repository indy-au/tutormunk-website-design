## Purpose

This playbook sets out how TutorMunk responds to a suspected or actual data breach affecting personal information we hold. It is written to align with the Privacy Act 1988 and the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme in Part IIIC of that Act, and to give the team a clear set of steps to follow under pressure.

This is a deliberately simple playbook suited to the size of the business today. It will be expanded as TutorMunk grows.

One point makes this different from an online business. TutorMunk holds the residential addresses of families with school-aged children, along with the days and times a tutor attends. If that information were exposed it could put a child at risk. Any breach involving address or schedule information is therefore treated as high risk from the outset.

## Scope

This playbook applies to any suspected or confirmed unauthorised access to, unauthorised disclosure of, or loss of personal information held by TutorMunk. That includes information about students, families, tutors and staff, whether held electronically, in email, or on paper such as signed session sheets and printed schedules.

## Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Data breach | Unauthorised access to, unauthorised disclosure of, or loss of personal information. |
| Eligible data breach | A data breach likely to result in serious harm to an individual. |
| Serious harm | Physical, psychological, emotional, financial or reputational harm. For TutorMunk this expressly includes any risk to the physical safety of a child. |
| Personal information | Has the meaning given in the Privacy Act 1988. |
| OAIC | The Office of the Australian Information Commissioner. |

## Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Breach lead | Accountable for the response, containment, assessment, notification decisions and any contact with the OAIC. This role is held by the owner, Indy Singh. |
| Tutors and staff | Report any suspected breach immediately on becoming aware of it. |

| Phase | Target |
|---|---|
| 1. Report | Immediately on becoming aware |
| 2. Contain | Within 24 hours of the report |
| 3. Investigate | Within 72 hours of becoming aware |
| 4. Assess | Within 30 days of becoming aware |

## Phase 1. Report

Anyone who becomes aware of a suspected data breach, including a tutor, a staff member, a parent or a third party, must report it immediately by email to hello@tutormunk.com.au with the subject line SUSPECTED DATA BREACH. If it is urgent, also phone 1300 00MUNK (1300 006 865).

The report should say, as far as it is known, what happened, when it happened or was noticed, what information or systems are affected, who may be affected, and what has already been done.

The breach lead logs the report in the Data Breach Register and opens an incident file.

Examples of what to report: a lost or stolen phone or laptop with family details on it, a printed schedule or signed session sheets left behind or lost, an email with family details sent to the wrong person, a suspected compromise of an email account, or a tutor continuing to hold family addresses after their engagement ended.

## Phase 2. Contain

Within 24 hours the breach lead takes containment steps proportionate to the incident, which may include:

- changing passwords and revoking access to accounts and shared files;
- remotely wiping or locking a lost device where possible;
- recalling an email sent in error and asking the recipient to delete it and confirm;
- recovering or securing paper records;
- removing a former tutor's access to any system or shared folder;
- preserving logs and evidence for the investigation.

Where a child's address may have been exposed, the breach lead considers immediately whether the affected family should be told straight away, ahead of the formal assessment, so they can take their own precautions.

## Phase 3. Investigate

Within 72 hours of becoming aware, the breach lead records in the incident file: the confirmed cause and timeline, the categories and volume of information involved, who is affected, whether the breach is ongoing, and an initial view on the likelihood of serious harm.

## Phase 4. Assess

Within 30 days of becoming aware, the breach lead completes a formal assessment of whether the breach is an eligible data breach. An eligible data breach requires unauthorised access, unauthorised disclosure or loss of personal information, that the breach is likely to result in serious harm to an individual, and that TutorMunk has not been able to prevent that risk through remedial action.

The assessment considers the kind of information involved and how sensitive it is, whether it was protected, who obtained it or may obtain it, the nature of the harm that could result, and any other relevant matter. Where children's addresses or session times are involved, the assessment starts from the position that serious harm is likely unless there is clear evidence otherwise.

## Phase 5. Notify

### If Notifiable

If the breach is assessed as an eligible data breach, TutorMunk notifies the affected individuals and the OAIC within 72 hours of that decision.

### How We Notify

- Affected individuals: by email, and by phone where the risk is to a child's safety.
- A notice on the TutorMunk website where individual notification is not practicable.
- The OAIC: through its online notifiable data breach form.

### What the Notification Says

The notification includes who we are and how to contact us, a description of the breach, the kinds of information involved, what we recommend the individual do, and what we have done to contain the breach and prevent it happening again.

### If Not Notifiable

If the breach is assessed as not notifiable, the breach lead records the reasoning in the incident file and keeps it for audit. We may still tell affected individuals where doing so is in their interests.

## Phase 6. Review and Close

Within 14 days of notification, or of closure where the breach was not notifiable, the breach lead completes a short review covering the root cause, how well containment worked, what will be changed to prevent a recurrence, any policy or training update needed, and closure of the incident file.

## Data Breach Register

TutorMunk keeps a Data Breach Register. Every suspected or confirmed breach is logged, whether or not it is notifiable. The register records the incident reference, the date we became aware, who reported it, a short description, what was affected, the assessment outcome, what notification was made, and the closure date. The register is kept for 7 years and reviewed at every six monthly policy review.

## Confidentiality

All communication about a breach is confidential and must be approved by the breach lead before it is sent. Staff and tutors must not discuss an incident externally or on social media. Affected people are treated with care and given clear, honest and practical information.
