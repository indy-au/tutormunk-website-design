Reading and writing are foundational skills that open the door to almost everything else in school. As a parent, the habits you build at home matter as much as anything that happens in the classroom. Here are practical, low-effort ways to support them.

1. **Fill the house with reading material.** Books and magazines around the house encourage a child to pick something up out of curiosity. Regular library visits, with your child choosing what interests them, help too.
2. **Let your child see you read.** Children pick up habits from what they see, not just what they are told. A novel, a newspaper or an ebook in your own hands sends a clearer message than any instruction.
3. **Read aloud together.** Taking turns reading aloud builds language and comprehension. Talking about the story and characters afterwards deepens understanding beyond just the words on the page.
4. **Give them a reason to write for pleasure.** A journal, a notebook for stories, or simple comics give a child a reason to write that has nothing to do with homework, which builds the habit without the pressure.
5. **Ask them to tell you about their day, in detail.** Recounting the day out loud builds the same skills as storytelling and speaking confidence, both of which carry over into writing.
6. **Set a consistent reading time.** A regular slot, before bed is common, turns reading into a habit rather than something that only happens when there is nothing else to do.
7. **Mix up the genres.** Fiction, nonfiction, mystery, adventure, letting a child try different kinds of books helps them work out what they actually enjoy, which keeps them reading.
8. **Talk about what they are reading.** Ask about favourite parts, characters and what they think will happen next. This builds comprehension and gets a child used to forming an opinion about a text, useful for later essay writing.
9. **Keep an eye on screen time.** Some screen time is fine, but it is worth making sure it is not crowding out the time available for books and writing.
10. **Get outside help if progress has genuinely stalled.** If reading or writing is a consistent struggle rather than a normal bump, a tutor can identify specifically what is holding a child back and work on that directly.

### In short

Building a strong reader and writer is less about any single trick and more about steady, low-pressure exposure: books around the house, reading together, a reason to write for fun, and genuine conversation about what your child is reading. Progress is not always linear, and that is normal.
