The after-school hours matter more than they get credit for. It is when children unwind, refuel and then, usually, tackle homework. Getting the eating and homework routine right in this window makes a real difference to how the rest of the evening goes.

### Part 1: the after-school snack

**Balanced snacking.** Children often come home hungry after a full day. A snack that combines some protein and carbohydrate, fruit, vegetables with dip, yoghurt, or wholegrain crackers with cheese, keeps energy steadier than something very sugary, which tends to cause a spike followed by a slump right when homework starts.

**Hydration.** Water matters more than it gets credit for. Encourage your child to drink water through the afternoon rather than only at meals, since dehydration affects concentration.

**A proper break to eat.** Sitting down to eat without a screen gives a genuine mental break between school and homework, and it is a natural moment to ask about their day.

### Part 2: the homework routine

**A designated space.** A quiet, well-lit spot with the basic supplies on hand cuts down on the time lost hunting for a pencil or a textbook, and helps signal that it is now homework time.

**A consistent schedule.** Some children work best straight after school, others need a short break first. Whichever suits your child, keep it consistent so homework becomes a predictable part of the day rather than a fight every afternoon.

**Breaks and movement.** Short breaks, a stretch, a walk around the yard, a few minutes outside, help prevent the fatigue that builds up during a long homework session. A longer break for the snack above also helps.

**Help without taking over.** Be available to answer questions and clarify instructions, but let your child do the work themselves. Praise effort and progress, not just the final grade, since that builds a steadier attitude toward difficult tasks.

**A clear finishing point.** Reviewing what was completed together, packing materials away, and talking briefly about tomorrow gives homework a proper end point rather than it dragging into the rest of the evening.

### In short

A balanced snack, proper hydration, a consistent homework space and schedule, and short breaks along the way go a long way toward making the after-school hours work better for everyone. Adjust the details to what actually suits your child, consistency matters more than any particular routine being "correct".
